SpoilerAlert

Blocks spoilers for anything you configure: a game, movie, TV show, book, or sports match. An item is hidden when it mentions a topic term (what you're avoiding) and a spoiler term (names, "ending", "dies"…), or any always-block phrase. Matching ignores case, accents, punctuation and extra spaces, and only matches whole words. One term per line.

The lists below start with a GTA 6 example. To protect something else, click Clear all lists, type your own terms, and click Save. You can keep several titles at once: every topic term is combined with every spoiler term.

Behavior

Display mode

The titles you don't want spoiled, with their common spellings, e.g. "GTA 6", "Dune Part Three", "Severance", "#SeveranceS3". Spaces inside a term are optional ("GTA 6" also matches "GTA6", "gta-6").

Character and actor names, places, and words like "ending", "dies", "twist", "finale", "leak". Terms of 4+ letters also match plurals ("mission" → "missions").

Hidden on their own, even without a topic term: leakers, spoiler accounts, or very specific phrases like "Mark S dies".

Look-alikes that should not count, e.g. earlier entries in a series ("GTA 5", "Dune Part Two"). They are ignored when looking for topic terms.

Paste a headline to see whether the current (unsaved) lists would hide it.

Where it runs

By default the shield only has access to YouTube, Reddit, Instagram and Facebook. To cover news sites and every other page (the generic fallback), allow access to all websites. Chrome will then warn that the extension can "read and change all your data on all websites". Nothing leaves your browser either way; see the README for the tradeoff.
